# Rule changes timeline

_Generated by `hwd gen-docs`._ Each entry is a patch to the rule book; mods can add, replace or remove entries (`rule_changes`).

| Season | Id | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 1946 | `baa-founded` | **Basketball Association of America tips off**: The BAA begins: 48-minute games, no shot clock, no three-point line, a reserve clause binding players to teams. |
| 1947 | `baa-48-games` | **Short season**: The 1947-48 season is cut to 48 games. |
| 1948 | `baa-60-games-again` | **Schedule back to 60**: Teams play 60 games. |
| 1949 | `nba-merger` | **The NBA is born**: The BAA absorbs the National Basketball League and becomes the NBA. Longer schedule, bigger rosters. |
| 1950 | `all-star-game` | **First All-Star Game**: The league holds its first All-Star Game (1951). |
| 1951 | `lane-12` | **Lane widened to 12 feet**: The key widens from 6 to 12 feet to slow down towering centers. |
| 1952 | `territorial-picks` | **Territorial draft picks**: Teams may claim a local college star instead of using a regular pick. |
| 1954 | `shot-clock` | **The 24-second shot clock**: Games had been collapsing into stalling and fouling. The 24-second clock arrives and scoring explodes. |
| 1958 | `round-sizes-58` | **Four-round... three-round playoffs**: Playoff formats settle into divisional rounds. |
| 1961 | `schedule-80` | **80-game schedule**: Expansion and growth push the schedule to 79-80 games. |
| 1962 | `schedule-80b` | **80 games**: The schedule becomes 80 games. |
| 1964 | `lane-16` | **Lane widened to 16 feet**: The key is widened again, to its modern 16 feet. |
| 1966 | `coin-flip` | **Draft order by coin flip**: The worst team in each conference flips a coin for the first pick. Territorial picks are abolished. |
| 1967 | `schedule-82` | **82-game season**: The schedule reaches the modern 82 games in 1967-68. |
| 1969 | `playoffs-7s` | **Best-of-seven rounds**: All playoff rounds are best-of-seven. |
| 1971 | `hardship` | **Hardship draft**: After the Spencer Haywood decision, players can leave college early to turn pro. |
| 1974 | `playoff-12` | **Twelve-team playoffs**: Bigger league, bigger playoffs, with a short first round. |
| 1975 | `hs-to-pro` | **High schoolers can turn pro**: The first teenagers jump from high school straight to the league. |
| 1976 | `free-agency-ofr` | **Free agency begins**: The Oscar Robertson settlement ends the reserve clause: players can switch teams after their option, but the old team may match offers. |
| 1979 | `three-point-line` | **The three-point line**: The three-point shot arrives in the NBA (23 ft 9 in), a gimmick that will eventually remake the sport. |
| 1981 | `bonus-5` | **Bonus at five fouls**: Teams go into the bonus earlier. |
| 1983 | `cap-agreement` | **Salary cap agreement**: Players and owners agree to the first salary cap, starting 1984-85, in exchange for revenue sharing. |
| 1984 | `salary-cap` | **The salary cap**: A soft salary cap begins, with the Larry Bird exception letting teams re-sign their own stars. |
| 1985 | `lottery` | **The draft lottery**: An envelope lottery sets the order for non-playoff teams. The draft stretches to seven rounds. |
| 1988 | `free-agency-modern` | **Modern free agency; mid-level exception**: Restricted and unrestricted free agency replace the right of first refusal. Teams get the mid-level exception and sign-and-trade deals. |
| 1989 | `draft-two-rounds` | **Two-round draft**: The draft is cut to two rounds. |
| 1990 | `weighted-lottery` | **Weighted lottery**: Odds now depend on record: the worst teams get the best chances. |
| 1994 | `short-three` | **Shorter three-point line**: The line moves in to 22 feet all around, and the lottery is revised. |
| 1995 | `rookie-scale` | **Rookie salary scale**: First-round rookie salaries are fixed by draft slot. The league adds Toronto and Vancouver. |
| 1997 | `line-restored` | **Three-point line restored**: The line goes back to 23 ft 9 in. |
| 1999 | `max-contract` | **Max contracts**: A new labor deal introduces maximum salaries tied to years of service. |
| 2001 | `zone-and-tax` | **Zone defense legal; luxury tax**: Illegal-defense rules are replaced by a defensive three-second rule, so zones are allowed. The luxury tax begins. A minor league (D-League) launches. |
| 2003 | `bo7-first-round` | **Best-of-seven first round**: Every playoff series is now best-of-seven. |
| 2004 | `no-hand-checking` | **Hand-checking banned**: Perimeter contact is called tightly; guards are freed to attack off the dribble. |
| 2005 | `age-limit` | **Age limit and 13 active**: Players must be 19 and a year out of high school. Teams may dress 13. |
| 2012 | `flop-penalty` | **Flopping penalties**: Fines for flopping begin. |
| 2014 | `replay-center` | **Replay center**: Referees get centralized video review. |
| 2016 | `intentional-fouls` | **Hack-a-player rules**: Intentional fouls away from the ball in the last two minutes are penalized. |
| 2017 | `two-way` | **Two-way contracts**: Teams can sign two players who shuttle between the league and its minor league. |
| 2018 | `freedom-of-movement` | **Freedom of movement; 14-second reset**: Referees crack down on contact; the shot clock resets to 14 after an offensive rebound. |
| 2019 | `coaches-challenge` | **Coach's challenge**: Coaches can challenge calls. |
| 2020 | `play-in` | **The play-in tournament**: Seeds 7-10 play for the final playoff spots. |
| 2023 | `aprons-and-cup` | **Aprons and in-season tournament**: A new labor deal adds punitive tax 'aprons' and an in-season tournament. |
